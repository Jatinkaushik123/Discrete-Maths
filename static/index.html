Discrete Mathematics Foundations & Proofs

1. Combinations vs. Permutations: Symmetry Elimination

Team selection is unordered: {s_a, s_b} = {s_b, s_a}. Searching permutations P(N, k) explores k! (120 for k=5) symmetrical sequences. Enforcing canonical index ordering i_1 < i_2 < ... < i_k cuts the search space strictly to C(N, k) combinations.

2. Internal-Node Early Pruning Mechanics

In naive brute force, constraints are evaluated as a post-filter at the leaf nodes after all C(N, k) subsets are materialized. In contrast, early pruning operates on internal nodes of the decision tree at depth m < k: pruning a partial prefix eliminates C(N - idx, k - m) potential leaves in a single O(1) step.

3. Conflict Graphs & Independent Sets

Let G_C = (V, E) be the conflict graph. A conflict-free team T is an Independent (Stable) Set in G_C. Candidate lookahead ensures no two vertices in T share an edge: ∀ u, v ∈ T (u ≠ v) ⇒ (u, v) ∉ E_C.

4. Multi-Objective Variance Penalty

Objective function: f(T) = TotalScore(T) - λ · Var(T). For two teams with sum 21, [5,5,5,5,1] has variance 2.56, while [4,4,4,4,5] has variance 0.16. With λ=1.5, the cohesive balanced team wins decisively.
